Educational overview

Understanding pneumonia.

Clear, general information about pneumonia, possible causes, common symptoms, risk factors, and when professional evaluation may be appropriate.

01
The condition

What is pneumonia?

Pneumonia is an infection or inflammatory condition of the lungs. It affects tiny air sacs called alveoli, which may fill with fluid or pus. This can interfere with oxygen exchange and make breathing more difficult.

It may affect one or both lungs, and its severity can vary widely depending on the cause, a person's overall health, age, and access to appropriate care.

02
Possible causes

Different organisms can be involved

Bacterial

Various bacteria may cause pneumonia, sometimes following another respiratory illness.

Viral

Respiratory viruses can cause viral pneumonia or contribute to secondary infection.

Other

Fungi and other organisms may be involved, particularly in specific risk groups.

03
Common symptoms

Symptoms can vary

  • Cough, which may be dry or produce mucus
  • Fever, chills, or sweating
  • Chest discomfort, especially with breathing or coughing
  • Shortness of breath or difficulty breathing
  • Fatigue, weakness, or reduced appetite

These symptoms can occur with many conditions and do not confirm pneumonia on their own.

04
Risk factors

Some people may be more vulnerable

Risk may be higher among very young children, older adults, people who smoke, and those with chronic heart or lung conditions, weakened immune systems, or recent hospitalization. Individual risk should be assessed by a healthcare professional.

05
General prevention

Risk reduction practices

  • Discuss appropriate vaccination with a healthcare professional
  • Practice regular hand and respiratory hygiene
  • Avoid smoking and unnecessary second-hand smoke exposure
  • Support general health with appropriate rest and nutrition
  • Follow infection-control guidance in healthcare settings
06
Professional care

When medical attention may be appropriate

Consider prompt professional medical evaluation for persistent fever or cough, worsening symptoms, chest discomfort, or breathing difficulty. Severe breathing trouble, confusion, bluish lips or face, or sudden deterioration may warrant urgent or emergency care according to local guidance.

If you are concerned about symptoms—especially in a child, older adult, or person with significant health conditions—contact a qualified healthcare professional.
